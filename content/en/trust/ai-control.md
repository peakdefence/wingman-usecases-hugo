## A change starts as a suggestion

When WINGMAN's AI, or an assistant connected to it, wants to change a record, it raises a suggestion. One suggestion is one change: one field, one link or one new record, with a description of what changes and why. Nothing happens until a person accepts or rejects it.

In a review, accepted suggestions are written only when the reviewer completes the review. Until then each one can be undone.

## Who can change what

Access roles decide what each person may read and change. An assistant connected over MCP signs in as a person and has that person's access, no more. A connection can be limited to reading.

People with write access can also change records directly, and so can an assistant they connect. If you let your assistant accept suggestions or change records, it does so as you, and the history shows your name. Choose which way of working fits each kind of record.

## Every change is recorded

Each change to a record is kept in its history: what changed, the value before and after, when, and who made it. The time is set by the system. Changes WINGMAN makes itself are recorded too. When it rewrites a record's brief, the history keeps the model that wrote it and when.

Documents and other attachments keep their versions.

## Findings cite their evidence

An audit finding is linked to the evidence it rests on, and each citation says how: supports conformity, indicates a nonconformity, or context. The auditor confirms or changes each finding.

## What AI does not do

- **It does not decide.** Accepting a suggestion, completing a review and approving a document are a person's actions, recorded with that person's name.
- **It does not create evidence.** Evidence is a record or a file someone collected. AI can point out that evidence is missing or has expired.
- **It does not send anything outside.** Reports, notices and reminders are drafted; a person approves and sends them.
- **It does not choose an auditor's sample or conclude an audit.** It can propose; the auditor decides.
- **It does not make a claimed control true.** A link between records shows that someone claimed it; only evidence shows it ran.
